## The pieces

### (73) An Analysis of Hegemonic Ideology — `Thesis-Hegemony-Incels`
**Research question:** *Is hegemony still relevant in the 21st century?*
The thesis argues that modern hegemonic ideology works less as a primer for consent —
the classical Gramscian reading — and more as a **deflective mechanism**: an amplifier of
manufactured cultural narratives that scapegoats minorities to divert attention from
systemic failures of capitalism. Two case studies: **Islamophobia in British
policy / political discourse**, and **the rise of incel subculture**.

### (75) Crimmigration: the criminalisation of migration — `Crimmigration-Analysis`
Why is migration treated as crime? Three theories are combined
into a single criminological picture — **critical race theory, labelling theory and
strain theory (Merton/Agnew)** — to show the criminalisation of migration as a racialised
labelling project embedded in British politics and media discourse.

### (71) COVID-19 and the collapse of the front/back stage — `Quan Analysis of COVID.pdf`
Quantitative report asking: *how has COVID-19 changed the way young people handle the
distinction between front and back stages, and what does that mean for social
interaction and mental well-being?* Uses wave 1 of a large UK longitudinal dataset
(n ≈ 2,077; multi-stage stratified random sampling) with a full variable table,
descriptive statistics and a candid limitations section, all interpreted through
Goffman's dramaturgical framework.

### (78) Race as hyperreality — `Race-As-Hyperreal`
Argues that race is a **social construction, not a biological essence** — a
"hegemonic manifestation" that operates as a *simulacrum* with no reference to anything
biologically real, yet which "becomes real" through its own construction. Moves from the
macro (colonialism, Foucauldian power-knowledge, the civilised/savage binary) to the
micro (Schutz–Sacks typification, intersubjectivity) to explain how the construction holds.

### (76) Language games and the power of the label — `Wittgensteinian-Foucauldian-Sociology`
Rebuilds labelling theory on two foundations: **Wittgenstein's language games** (labels
are moves in socially governed language practices, not neutral descriptions) and a
**Foucauldian hegemonic analysis of the power inside those games** — the "hegemonic power
of the label."  

### (70) Whose ageing is it anyway? — `Qual Research Ageism.pdf`
Qualitative research report on whether ideas about age and ageing have become more
positive or negative over time. Method: close reading of Dr Lorna Warren's published
work (University of Sheffield) plus a **semi-structured interview**, framed by a
constructivist social ontology in which older people are the experts on their own ageing.
Includes literature review, method, analysis and a self-critical limitations section.

### (75) The commodity: value, exploitation, alienation — `Commodities-Capitalism`
A walk through the first chapter of *Das Kapital* Vol. I: the duality of **use-value and
exchange-value**, commodity fetishism, and how the labour theory of value leads to
**exploitation**, the **tendency of the rate of profit to fall**, and finally the
**alienation** of the labourer from their work — with the formal relations (s/v, organic
composition of capital) stated explicitly.

### (76) Consumerism as dramaturgy — `Goffmanian-Consumerism`
The claim: the consumer sociologies of **Veblen and Bourdieu are instances of Goffmanian
macrosociology**. Conspicuous consumption and taste-as-distinctive-capital are re-read as
dramaturgical performance — front/back regions, impression management, staging talk —
arguing that the mechanics underneath Veblen and Bourdieu are Goffman's.
